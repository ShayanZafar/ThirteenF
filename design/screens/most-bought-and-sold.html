Method

Net 13F flow is the change in shares held by every 13F filer, valued at the quarter-end price. 13F filings show long positions in US-listed securities only: no shorts, no cash.

Est. value is the range of three models: discounted cash flow, EV/EBIT against the company’s own 10-year median, and price to free cash flow against peers. The gap is measured to the middle of that range.

Method

Ranked by net 13F buying or selling as a share of shares outstanding: the change in shares held by every 13F filer between Mar 31 and Jun 30, 2026. Dollar amounts use the Jun 30 price. “Led by” names the manager with the largest change in shares.

Priced in? compares today’s price with the est. value range. For bought stocks it reads Yes once the price is inside or above the range, Partly once the price has covered at least a quarter of the distance from its Jun 30 level to the middle of the range, and No otherwise. Sold stocks mirror this: Yes once the price is inside or below the range.

Index funds count toward every total and every manager count. Their buying follows money coming into the funds and changes to index weights rather than a view on one company, so the Index funds column also shows it on its own: their net buying as a share of shares outstanding, and how much of the total it makes up.

“Since Aug 14” is the move after the last Q2 filings were due, when every position was public. 13F positions are dated to quarter end, so part of a move can happen before anyone could see the filings.
