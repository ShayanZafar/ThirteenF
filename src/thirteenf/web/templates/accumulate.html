Method

A stock is on the list when four things hold. More funds held it, against the tide, in each of the last {{ fmt.count(streak) }} periods or longer: its change in funds holding beat the change in all 13F filers. Its net 13F flow, the shares funds bought minus the shares they sold at the quarter-end price, was positive. Its free cash flow was positive in each of its last two fiscal years. And the lower of those two years was at least {{ min_yield }}% of its market value.

Free cash flow is operating cash flow minus capital spending, from the company’s 10-K filings in the SEC’s XBRL financial data. Taking the lower of two years keeps one unusual year, such as a big swing in inventory, from topping the list. Market value is the quarter-end price funds reported (the median value per share across all funds holding it) times the diluted share count for the latest fiscal year. Price since the quarter’s start is the change from the last close before funds’ buying quarter began to the latest daily close, from {{ fmt.price_sources(prices.sources) if prices else "daily prices" }}{% if not prices %} (not loaded yet: add a free Stooq key to data/settings.toml and run update-data; until then the column shows the quarter’s change in the price funds reported){% endif %}. Beside it, how far that is from the market’s move over the same days (SPY, an S&P 500 fund): a pick lagging the market by {{ lag_pts }} points or more while funds kept buying has not priced that flow in yet; one ahead by as much already has. Rank by Price lagging to put those first. Under the net 13F flow, how large funds’ net buying was against the value traded in the quarter: a flow worth a few percent of trading moves a price more than one worth a fraction of a percent.

Left out: banks, insurers and lenders (companies that report deposits, interest income or insurance premiums, or whose loan interest and fees are a quarter or more of revenue), whose operating cash flow carries deposits, loans and premiums; companies that report in IFRS (most foreign issuers and ADRs) or report no capital spending; companies whose latest fiscal year ended more than 18 months before the period; and stocks whose share count fails a check: the diluted count and the latest cover-page count differ by more than 1.5 times (usually a split since the fiscal year end), or funds’ 13F holdings are worth more than 1.5 times the market value. Only stocks held by {{ fmt.count(minimum) }} or more funds at both dates are compared.

The list is rebuilt each time update-data runs, so it moves with every new quarter of 13F filings and every new 10-K. It is a starting point for your own research, not advice.
